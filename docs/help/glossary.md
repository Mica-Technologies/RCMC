# Glossary

**Air gate**
:   A gate along a coaster platform's edge. It opens while a train loads and shuts before it leaves, and
    the train waits for it.

**Airtime**
:   The floating feeling of negative vertical G, over a hill taken at speed. The thing coaster fans chase.

**Arrival board**
:   The large amber sign hung over a metro platform, showing the trains due in each direction.

**Bank**
:   How far the track is tilted sideways. A banked turn tilts into the curve, so riders are pushed into
    their seats rather than thrown sideways.

**Berth**
:   One platform of a metro station, on one track. An island platform has two berths, one on each side.

**Block, block section**
:   A stretch of track that may hold only one train at a time. Coasters use block sections to run several
    trains; metro lines use signals, which work the same way.

**Block brake**
:   A coaster brake that slows every passing train to a crawl and ends a block, so a train can be held
    there while the block ahead is occupied.

**Catenary**
:   The overhead wires above an electric railway. One of the metro track styles.

**Chain lift**
:   A stretch of coaster track that hauls the train up at a steady speed. It gives the coaster its height,
    which it spends as speed.

**Circuit**
:   Track that closes into a loop, so a train can go round for ever.

**Dispatch**
:   Sending a coaster train out of the station. Automatic, or by pressing DISPATCH on the operator panel.

**Drive on sight**
:   How metro trains keep apart without signals: each stops 5 blocks short of any train ahead of it.

**Dwell**
:   How long a train waits at a station: with its doors open on a metro, with its gates open on a coaster.

**Element**
:   A piece of ride hardware on the track: a lift, a launch, brakes, drive tyres, a station.

**G, G-force**
:   How hard a ride pushes you, in multiples of normal gravity. 1 g is normal. **Vertical** G pushes you
    into or out of your seat, **lateral** G sideways, **longitudinal** G forwards or back.

**Headway**
:   The least time between two metro trains leaving the same platform in the same direction.

**Heartline**
:   The line through the riders' chests. RCMC's inversions roll around it, so riders turn over without
    being thrown sideways.

**Hold**
:   Keeping a metro train at its next station, doors open, until it's released.

**Inversion**
:   An element that turns riders upside down: a loop, corkscrew, zero-g roll, Immelmann or dive loop.

**Island platform**
:   One platform with a track on each side. One station, two berths.

**Launch**
:   A stretch of coaster track that pushes the train with motors, instead of hauling it up with a chain.

**Line**
:   A metro route: its stations in order, and what trains do at the ends.

**Loop, shuttle, turnback**
:   What a metro line does at its ends. A **loop** line runs round a circuit and never ends. A **shuttle**
    line reverses at each end. A **turnback** line runs on two tracks joined by a turning loop at each
    end, so trains never reverse.

**Node**
:   A point you place that the track passes through. The track is a smooth curve through its nodes.

**Operator panel**
:   The console that runs a coaster: open, close, dispatch, emergency stop, trains and hardware.

**Line Control Desk**
:   The console that runs a metro line: its trains, holds, dwell and headway.

**Pass-through**
:   How many times a shuttle coaster's station lets the train through before catching it.

**Ride check**
:   A simulated lap that finds where a coaster is too intense, or where a train can't get past.

**Section**
:   One continuous piece of track with its own id. A coaster is usually one section; sections can be
    joined, and joined by switches.

**Segment type**
:   What a span of track is: plain track, chain lift, launch, brakes, station and so on.

**Service**
:   A metro train running a line under its own control.

**Signal**
:   A boundary between two blocks on a metro line. A train won't run past it into a block another train
    is in.

**Span**
:   The stretch of track between two neighbouring nodes. Segment types and edits apply to spans.

**Stall, valleying**
:   A train that ran out of speed and stopped out on the track, because it couldn't get over something.

**Switch**
:   Where one track divides into two or more. Trains take whichever way it is set.

**Track style**
:   How a section of track looks: the coaster look, or one of the metro looks.

**Transfer track**
:   Coaster track that slides a train sideways onto a storage track, to take it out of service.
